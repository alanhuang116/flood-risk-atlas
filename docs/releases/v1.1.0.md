Climate-adjusted flood damage estimates for the 110.7 million residential buildings in the USACE National
Structure Inventory 2026, aggregated to 33,110 ZIP codes (2020 ZCTA). Fluvial, pluvial and coastal flooding;
present climate and SSP2-4.5 / SSP5-8.5 at 2030 and 2060; average annual loss (AAL) and conditional 1-in-100 and
1-in-1,000-year loss, in US dollars and as damage ratios. Built from public data only.

**Platform:** https://alanhuang116.github.io/flood-risk-atlas/ ·
**Methodology:** [docs/methodology.md](https://github.com/alanhuang116/flood-risk-atlas/blob/main/docs/methodology.md)

Present climate by flood type: fluvial 7.9, pluvial 15.6, coastal 2.0. The combined figure takes the deepest
flood type at each home, so it is below the sum of the three.

### Changes since v1.0

* **Post-FIRM elevation.** Homes in FEMA high-risk zones (A and V) built after the first flood map have their
  lowest floor at or above the base flood elevation and no basement. The share of such homes in each block
  group, and how high their floors sit, come from 1.92 million NFIP policies (2019).
* **State calibration factors.** One factor per state on top of the national calibration to NFIP claims. Each
  state is pulled towards its NOAA climate region, more strongly when its claims come from a few large events.
  The method was chosen by testing on held-out loss years. 38 of 47 states are now within a factor of two of
  their claims, against 20 with the national calibration alone.
* **Raised-crest defence scenario.** Future scenarios are also run with levees raised to keep their design
  standard. New field `defence_scenario`: `fixed-crest` or `raised-crest`.
* **Fix.** The FEMA zone code `AREA NOT INCLUDED` (no flood map) was treated as an A zone in v1.0. This
  affected 0.78 million homes.

### Limitations

* The model is calibrated to NFIP insured losses. The insured-equivalent ratio is 0.89 (0.98 on held-out states)
  and the county rank correlation is 0.75.
* Outside FEMA high-risk zones, flood insurance take-up is 1–2%. Losses there are only weakly constrained by
  claims.
* State factors are statistical, so estimates can change sharply at state borders.
